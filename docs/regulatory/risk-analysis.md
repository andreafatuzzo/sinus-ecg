_Inspired by ISO 14971:2019. Version 0.4.3, 2026-10-10. Status: Milestone 2; version 0.4.1 confirmed by the project owner on 2026-10-07 (the open point cited by version 0.4.1 opened by decision of the project owner on 2026-10-07); the known anomaly of version 0.4.2 recorded by decision of the project owner on 2026-10-09; version 0.4.3 records the Milestone 2 results, approved by the project owner on 2026-10-11._

## Conventions

- Hazards are identified as `HAZ-001`, risk controls as `RC-001`.
- A risk control implemented in software links to the `SRS-xxx` requirement(s) that implement it, and each of those requirements lists the control.
- Every change that affects behavior is reviewed against this document.
- User-level hazards come from the use scenarios in [`functional-analysis.md`](functional-analysis.md); use-related hazards are also listed in [`usability.md`](usability.md).
- IDs HAZ-008, HAZ-009, RC-009 and RC-010 remain reserved for battery overheating and skin reaction to electrodes (OP-025), analysed with the hardware at Milestone 4. HAZ-006, HAZ-007, RC-007 and RC-008, reserved in v0.2 for electrode contact loss and stale data, are now used for those hazards.
- A risk control whose implementing requirements belong to a later milestone names that milestone and its open point. Its hazard is acceptable only once those requirements are written and verified.

## Scope

- **Intended use and foreseeable misuse:** see [`safety-class.md`](safety-class.md). The main foreseeable misuse is reading Sinus output as health information.
- **Covered now:** hazards arising from the system as a whole, from the Milestone 1 software (offline filtering, QRS detection, validation and golden-vector export), from the Milestone 2 software (real-time signal conditioning and beat detection, heart-rate tracking, signal quality index, and their equivalence with the reference), and from published or exported results. Hazards of the later milestones that can already be stated from the functional analysis (signal quality, stale data, low battery, device faults, replay, abstention) are listed with their controls; their implementing requirements are written at the milestone that introduces them.
- **To be analysed at the milestone that introduces them:**
  - wireless link: corruption, reordering and unauthorised access (Milestone 4, OP-012; security in `cybersecurity.md`);
  - real-time latency and sampling jitter (Milestone 4, OP-014);
  - battery overheating and skin reaction (Milestone 4, OP-025);
  - stored personal data, authentication and privacy (Milestone 5, OP-016);
  - beat classification as a whole, and the review of the safety classification (Milestone 6, OP-017).

## Scales

**Severity**

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| S1 Negligible | Inconvenience or temporary discomfort |
| S2 Minor | Temporary anxiety, an unnecessary medical visit, or a short delay in seeking care for a non-urgent condition |
| S3 Serious | Injury or impairment requiring medical intervention, or a significant delay in treating an urgent condition |
| S4 Critical | Death or permanent impairment |

**Probability of harm** (the software itself is assumed to fail; this is the probability that a failure leads to harm)

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| P1 Improbable | Requires several unlikely events together |
| P2 Remote | Could happen, but is unlikely over the life of the project |
| P3 Occasional | Likely to happen sometimes |

**Acceptability:** S1 is acceptable at any probability. S2 is acceptable at P1–P2. S3 is acceptable only at P1. S4 is acceptable only at P1, and only when the reduction to P1 comes from an inherent-safety-by-design control that is verified by inspection; otherwise S4 is not acceptable.

S3 for HAZ-001/002 would require an alarm or diagnostic claim. The intended use excludes both (see [`safety-class.md`](safety-class.md)), so the worst credible harm is rated S2.

**Probabilities of HAZ-001 and HAZ-002 with the Milestone 1 results.** These probabilities were first estimated without data. They were revisited with the Milestone 1 validation report ([`docs/validation/qrs-ec57-report.md`](../validation/qrs-ec57-report.md), SRS-007 and SRS-014), and the levels are unchanged:
- **Measured.** On the 48 records of the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database (91285 scored reference beats), detection misses 251 beats (gross Se 99.73%) and makes 158 false detections (gross +P 99.83%): about one beat in 360 is missed and one detection in 580 is false, and both SRS-007 targets are met. The failures are concentrated: 7 records are below 99.5% in Se or +P. In those records, most missed beats are early ectopic or aberrated beats, often in atrial fibrillation or flutter, or lie in stretches where the QRS complex is very small on the recorded lead; most false detections lie in a few stretches with noise or artefact, or with atrial fibrillation or flutter. With electrode motion noise added (noise stress test), detection is unchanged at 24 dB and 18 dB SNR and degrades below, mostly through false detections: gross Se and +P are 99.89% and 95.69% at 12 dB, 98.43% and 79.16% at 6 dB, 93.51% and 69.79% at 0 dB, and 82.11% and 62.97% at −6 dB.
- **Before risk control: P3.** Missed and false beats occur in practice, as the figures show even with RC-001 in place, and they cluster in irregular rhythms and noisy stretches, which is where a user is most likely to read the output as information about their heart.
- **After risk control: P2.** The validated detection (RC-001), the published weak points (RC-004) and the labelling (RC-005) make harm unlikely: isolated missed or false beats change a heart rate computed over several beats little (at most 5 bpm for an isolated missed or extra beat, SRS-025, RC-018), and nothing raises an alarm or interprets the rhythm. P1 is not claimed, because the failures concentrate in the irregular rhythms that a user might want to check. False detections in a signal of low quality, as at the lower SNR levels above, are also the hazardous situation of HAZ-006, controlled by RC-007.
- **Milestone 2 results.** (RC-012) The real-time library reproduces the reference on the golden vectors on the computer (largest differences 1.8e-6 mV, 0 samples of detection index, 7.5e-6 bpm, 7.5e-7 of signal quality index) and on the ESP32-S3 in an emulator (32 of 32 vectors), and on the 60 records of the databases it gives the same detections and counts per record as the reference. The SRS-007 figures above (gross Se 99.73%, +P 99.83%) therefore hold for the real-time library. (RC-017) Detection right after the start-up period, measured on the real recordings (SRS-023): gross Se 99.70% and +P 99.80%, after a correction of the measurement method approved by the project owner, a little below the figures of SRS-007 over the whole records. (RC-018) The heart-rate accuracy and tolerance criteria are verified on the reference and on the library. The estimates of HAZ-001 and HAZ-002 are not changed: these results show that the library performs as the reference and that the figures after the start-up period stay within 0.03 points of those of SRS-007, which does not move the failures that justify P2 (irregular rhythms, noisy stretches), and they come from the same databases, so they add no evidence on unseen recordings or on the worn device.
- **Limits of the evidence.** The figures come from the detection design fixed before the first run: no parameter or rule was changed after the results were seen, and none is changed in Milestones 1 and 2 (`sdp.md` §6; decisions on OP-056). They still come from the databases used during development, recorded with other equipment and leads than the Sinus device, so they do not show how detection performs on unseen recordings or on the worn device (OP-029). The scored part of the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database has no interval shorter than 250 ms between consecutive reference beats, and its ventricular flutter and fibrillation episodes are not scored, so SRS-007 gives no evidence on QRS complexes that follow each other more closely, the HAZ-001 cause stated with the range of SRS-006. No minimum detection result is set per SNR: the signal quality index must mark the degraded levels as not usable instead (SRS-029, RC-007); further noise types: OP-031. The heart-rate criteria of RC-018, used above, cover a regular rhythm, a change between regular rhythms and an isolated missed or extra beat; the heart rate in irregular rhythms is not specified yet (OP-073, Milestone 6). **Known anomaly: the signal quality index does not meet SRS-029 on three of its 11 criteria** (OP-074, Milestone 3), on the real databases: at 6 dB 24.31% of the windows are marked usable (limit at most 20%; records 118e06 and 119e06: 21.6% and 27.0%); for the electrode motion noise record 14.37% (limit at most 10%; 246 of its windows have an index between 0.45 and 0.50, just under the usable threshold); the median index is 0.3455 at 0 dB and 0.3566 at −6 dB, so it increases as the SNR falls, with a flat floor around 0.35. The other criteria are met (records 118 and 119 of the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database 100% usable; 24 dB and 18 dB 100%; 0 dB 1.73%; −6 dB 9.56%; baseline wander record 2.84%; muscle artefact record 3.95%; the 12 dB level, without a criterion, 84.28%). The threshold was fixed before the evaluation and is not re-tuned (decision of the project owner, 2026-10-09). Effect on HAZ-002 and HAZ-006: more degraded windows than allowed can be marked usable at 6 dB and for electrode motion, so RC-007 does not yet reach its stated effect there. The estimates are not changed: the shortfall is 4.3 points over the limit at 6 dB, and 4.4 points at the electrode motion record, 75.7% and 85.6% of those windows are still marked not usable, nearly all windows are marked not usable at 0 dB and below, and the limits of SRS-029 are verification criteria, not values derived from the probabilities. The acceptability of HAZ-006 therefore stays conditional on RC-007 being verified, which is not the case for SRS-029 until OP-074 is closed. The index does not grade the levels below about 0 dB, so it must not be presented as a graded measure of quality (OP-072).

HAZ-005 is rated S4 because the worst credible harm of mains current across the chest is death. It is controlled in hardware, not by software (see `safety-class.md` §Rationale, point 4), and its acceptability depends on the inspection of the schematic (OP-013).

## Residual risk

All hazards are acceptable after their risk controls; for HAZ-005 this holds once RC-006 is verified (OP-013, OP-024, OP-038). For HAZ-006, HAZ-007 and HAZ-011 to HAZ-013 it holds once the requirements implementing their controls are written and verified at the milestones named above; HAZ-014 is re-assessed with beat classification (OP-017). The probabilities of HAZ-001 and HAZ-002 after risk control take no credit for RC-017 and RC-018 beyond what the Milestone 1 figures show: their requirements are verified in Milestone 2 (except as stated for SRS-029), and the results are reported above (Milestone 2 results). For HAZ-006, SRS-029 is not met on three criteria (OP-074, Milestone 3): the acceptability holds on the measured figures stated in the limits of the evidence and is confirmed when OP-074 is closed. The overall residual risk is acceptable **for the stated intended use**, and depends on RC-005: using Sinus as a health monitor falls outside this analysis.
